# Spartan Engine License FAQ

Plain-language answers about the [Spartan Engine License 2.0](license.md). This
page is guidance only; if it ever disagrees with the license, the license wins.

## The short version

| Who | What you pay |
| --- | --- |
| Hobbyists, students, noncommercial projects | Nothing |
| Organizations under $25M a year (revenue plus funding) | Nothing until a product earns $1M, then 3% of revenue above $1M |
| Organizations at $25M a year or more | Custom agreement, get in touch |
| Selling Spartan itself as an engine, SDK, or hosted service | Custom agreement, get in touch |

## Do I need to contact you before selling my game?

No, as long as your organization is under $25M a year. The standard commercial
license applies automatically. You only need to get in touch once a product
passes $1M in lifetime gross revenue.

## I'm not sure if my project counts as commercial. What should I do?

Don't worry about it. If you're under $25M a year, commercial use is free until a
product earns $1M, so the distinction rarely matters. Patreon, donations, ads,
monetized videos, and store sales are all fine.

## Is the $1M threshold per product or per company?

Per product, over its whole lifetime. DLC, expansions, ports, and new editions
count toward the same product. A sequel is a new product with its own threshold.

## What counts as gross revenue?

Everything the product brings in: sales, in-app purchases, subscriptions, ads,
sponsorship, crowdfunding, publisher advances, and paid services. Store fees are
not deducted, but refunds, chargebacks, and sales taxes or VAT are.

## Why is the royalty based on revenue before store fees?

It keeps reporting simple and matches how other engines charge. The rate is set
lower than common engine royalties to account for it.

## Can I pay once instead of paying royalties?

Yes. Get in touch and we can agree a one-time buyout for a product.

## My studio grew past $25M. Do my shipped games change terms?

No. Products you already shipped keep the standard terms. New products need a
custom agreement.

## Can you change the terms on me later?

Not for a product you've already shipped. Each product keeps the terms of the
license version it was first released under, for its lifetime.

## We're a large studio. Can we evaluate the engine first?

Yes. Development, prototyping, and evaluation are free for everyone. You need an
agreement before commercial release or production use.

## Can I use parts of the code in my own engine or game?

Yes. Noncommercial use is free. Commercial use follows the same rules as using
the whole engine. Keep the copyright notice and the license with the code.

## Do I need a splash screen or logo?

No. Keep the copyright notice and license in your documentation or a legal
notices screen.

## What about versions released under MIT?

Copies and versions that were released under the MIT License keep those
permissions.

## I contributed to Spartan. What happens to my code?

You keep the copyright. By contributing, you allow Panos Karabelas to license your
contribution under these terms, including commercial agreements.
